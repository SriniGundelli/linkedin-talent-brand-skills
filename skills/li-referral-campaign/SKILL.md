# li-referral-campaign

Referrals are usually the best-quality, fastest hires, and most programmes die
because the ask is "please share this link". People will share when it is easy,
specific and safe.

> Also apply the **House rules** section of `~/.claude/linkedin/voice.md`.
> Nothing is posted or sent without the owner's yes.

## Step 1: make the role easy to refer

Ask for the role facts: outcome, range, location, the three things the right
person has done, and the referral bonus and its rules. Then write a **"who to
think of" card** employees can read in 15 seconds:

```
Looking for someone who has {done X} and wants {Y}.
Sounds like: {titles they might have now}.
Range: {range}. Where: {location/work model}.
Bonus: {amount, when paid}.
```

Employees refer people they can picture. Titles and "has done" lines beat a job
description.

## Step 2: the internal ask (Slack/email, not LinkedIn)

Draft a short message from a leader: why this role matters, the card, the
bonus, the one action requested (name two people, not "share"), and the date
the push ends. Make sharing optional and thank people for any outcome.

## Step 3: the share kit (do not hand out one post)

Identical posts from twenty accounts look like spam and are ignored. Produce
**five distinct post angles** the employee picks from and edits:

1. **Personal reason**: "Here is why I would take this role if I were looking."
2. **The problem**: the hard thing the hire will own.
3. **The team**: who they would work with and how it runs.
4. **The ask**: "If this sounds like someone you worked with, send it on."
5. **The numbers**: range, location, stage, process length.

Each in two lengths (short and standard), each with a blank for the employee's
own detail (`{{your reason}}`). Never write the employee's detail for them.

## Step 4: the one-to-one DM

For employees who know a specific person, a script that references why they
thought of them, the range, and a no-pressure ask. Remind them to message only
people they know, one at a time, by hand.

## Step 5: track it

A simple table the owner can keep: referrer, candidate (initials), date,
stage, outcome, bonus status. No automation of LinkedIn, no scraping contacts
from the platform.

## Never

- Never pressure employees or track who shared. Participation is voluntary.
- Never promise a bonus the policy does not state.
- Never let anyone message a person who has asked not to be contacted.
- Never post pay, reviews or private details about a named employee or
  candidate without their consent.

## Output

The who-to-think-of card, the internal ask, the five-angle share kit in two
lengths, the DM script, and the tracker table.
